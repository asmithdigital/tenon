The trade-off nobody wrote down

Discovery is the research and validation work that happens before something gets designed — the step that checks a proposed solution actually addresses the real problem, rather than the assumed one. It is also the easiest thing in any studio to skip under deadline pressure, because skipping it rarely causes a visible problem on the day. It usually causes one three months later, by which point nobody remembers that the shortcut was ever taken, let alone who decided to take it.

We used to lose that decision the same way most teams do: quietly. A designer would agree to move straight to delivery because the timeline demanded it, the trade-off would live in their head for a while, and then it would evaporate. If the shortcut turned out fine, nobody thought about it again. If it didn't, there was no record that the risk had ever been named — the consequences landed on whoever executed the shortcut, not on whoever set the timeline that forced it.

The fix we landed on isn't a rule that blocks work from proceeding — rules like that get worked around the first time they're inconvenient. It's a standing weekly session where unfinished work gets put in front of the whole team, and three questions get asked out loud, every time, before anything moves forward: does this actually need the team's attention, has discovery happened, and if it hasn't, who is deciding to skip it and why. That third answer gets written down — not to assign blame, but to build a record. A trade-off made once is a judgement call. The same trade-off made silently, every week, for six months, is a resourcing problem that nobody can prove exists without a written history of it happening.

The clearest sign this became infrastructure rather than a habit tied to one person: when someone who ran the session left, it kept running without them, unchanged. Nothing about it depended on any single person's memory or presence. That's the test we'd suggest for any process like this — not whether it works while you're watching it, but whether it survives you leaving the room.
